Privacy Policy for Orbitfire

Last updated: 14 October 2026 · Package com.uporat.orbitblaster

Orbitfire is a single-player arcade game. It has no accounts, no sign-in, and no social features. I do not collect, store or receive any personal information about you. The only data that leaves your device is collected by Google's advertising service, and this page explains exactly what that is.

What stays on your device

The game saves a small amount of progress locally, using Android's standard app storage. It never leaves your phone, it is not backed up to me, and uninstalling the app deletes it:

  • Highest level unlocked
  • Best score
  • Total targets destroyed
  • The stars you have earned on each level
  • Your daily challenge streak, best streak and best daily score, your latest daily result, and whether you are holding a streak shield
  • Whether sound is on
  • Whether vibration is on
  • Whether you have already seen the meteor-storm tip
  • Whether the game has already asked you for a rating, and how many levels you have completed, so that it asks at most once and not too early

That is the complete list. There is nothing else.

Advertising

Orbitfire shows ads through Google AdMob, and this is the only part of the app that sends anything anywhere. There are no ads during play. An ad can appear in three places:

  • A full-screen ad between levels, at most every few levels.
  • An optional ad you choose to watch from the boost menu, in exchange for an in-game boost.
  • After you finish the daily challenge, an offer to watch an ad in exchange for a streak shield, which keeps your daily streak alive if you miss a day. The offer explains the reward first and has a No thanks button; if you do not choose either option, the ad starts after a short countdown. Choosing No thanks closes the offer with no ad at all.

To serve those ads, Google may collect and process:

  • Your device's advertising ID — a resettable identifier Android provides for this purpose
  • Device information such as model, operating system version and language
  • Your IP address, and the approximate location derived from it
  • Whether an ad was shown, viewed or tapped

This is collected by Google, not by me. I never see it, and I receive nothing beyond anonymous aggregate counts of how many ads were shown.

Google uses this data to select ads and to measure them, which may include personalised advertising depending on your consent and your device settings. Google's own policies govern what happens to it:

  • How Google uses information from apps that use its services
  • Google Privacy Policy

Your choices about ads

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the app shows you a consent form the first time it runs, and your answer decides whether ads are personalised. You can change that answer at any time from the app's settings.

Anywhere in the world, Android itself gives you control. In Settings → Privacy → Ads you can reset your advertising ID, which disconnects it from everything previously associated with it, or delete it entirely, which stops apps receiving one at all.

Sharing

The game has share buttons: tell a friend about the game, share a score card after a level, or share your daily challenge result. Each one opens Android's own share sheet, and you choose the app the message goes to. The game itself sends nothing to me or to anyone else when you share.

Shared messages include a link to the game on Google Play. The link carries a short label saying which button it came from (for example daily), so Google Play can show me how many installs came from each kind of share. The label contains nothing about you.

Permissions

Orbitfire requests no runtime permissions. It does not ask for your camera, microphone, contacts, files, or precise location, and it cannot access them. Two permissions are declared automatically by the Google Mobile Ads library and are used only for advertising: internet access, and access to the advertising ID.

Children

Orbitfire is not directed at children under 13, and I do not knowingly collect any information from them. If you believe a child has provided information through this app, please contact me at the address below and I will look into it.

Data I hold about you

None. Because I collect nothing and store nothing on a server, there is no account to close, no export to request and no records to delete. Everything the game knows about you is on your own device, and uninstalling removes it. Requests about data held by Google should go to Google, using the links above.

Changes to this policy

If the app's data practices change, this page will be updated and the date at the top will change with it. Material changes will also be noted in the app's release notes on Google Play.
